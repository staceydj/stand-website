Project brief

Social Transformation Action Defined (STAND), South Africa

Track Her Case

Following every case of violence against women to justice

A live case tracking system in the Overstrand, Western Cape, that follows each report through the police, hospitals, social services and the courts until it is resolved.

The challenge

South Africa has some of the strongest laws on gender-based violence in the world. The services exist and the mandates exist. What breaks is the handover between them.

A woman reports violence to the police and her case stalls before it reaches the prosecutor. She is referred for medical care and social services never hear of her. By the time a case reaches court it is often incomplete, and it is struck from the roll for want of clarity rather than merit.

These gaps fall hardest on marginalised and underserved women, who are the most likely to need every service at once.

Our solution

The Red Dot Process, designed by STAND with Overstrand Municipality and the Overberg Violence Prevention Unit, with the support of the DG Murray Trust and AIDSFONDS, runs on the municipality's existing Citizen App. Each case receives a private reference number, so no names are recorded, and every handover is mapped across police, prosecutors, the courts, health, social development and child protection services.

A community layer of 31 trained volunteer ambassadors supports survivors on the ground and helps them follow their cases through every service. We equip them with skills and practical support, including transport to get survivors to the police, clinics, social services and court.

How it works

Why now

South Africa has classified gender-based violence as a national disaster and committed billions to more social workers and courts. More services mean more handovers, and every handover is a place a case can be lost. Track Her Case is live and ready to scale; your gift keeps it running while institutional funding is secured.

Long-term impact

Every case followed to its outcome shows where the system holds and where it can be strengthened. Over time this builds a district-wide evidence base that helps police, prosecutors and health and social services work as one system, so more cases reach court complete and more women see justice.

Who we are

STAND is a women-led South African organisation working where justice, health and protection meet. Since 2018 we have trained more than 500 officials and practitioners, reached more than 700 women and community members directly, trained 29 INTERPOL officers from 11 Southern African states, and managed more than R16.9 million in donor funding, under an independent Board of Directors.
