About STAND
A specialist technical and implementation partner.
We are a women-led technical and implementation partner working where justice, health, protection, and implementation meet.
Who we are
STAND (Social Transformation Action Defined) is a South African women-led nonprofit, a registered Public Benefit Organisation with Section 18A tax-exemption status, and a specialist technical platform working at the intersection of gender-responsive justice, gender-based violence, substance-related harms, public health, and systems accountability.
Founded in 2018 and based in Cape Town, STAND operates as a lean, technically deep organisation, mobilising specialist consultants and collaborators according to programme scope and need. This model keeps STAND agile and responsive while holding the analytical and technical standards of a senior institutional partner.
We are not a generalist development organisation. Our work is intersectional by design, sitting where gender-based violence, substance-related harms, justice and health meet, and we bring the expertise to close the gap between what the evidence shows and what actually gets done.
Four things distinguish how we work
01
We work in the hard middle ground.
Policy commitments exist. Services are theoretically available. Institutions are willing. And yet systems fail. STAND operates in exactly the space where this happens, with the evidence, relationships, and implementation experience to address it.
02
Our credibility is earned, not claimed.
STAND's positioning rests on documented work: assessments completed, training delivered, referral pathways designed, tools produced, coordination systems strengthened. We do not inflate. Every capacity we offer has been demonstrated.
03
We design for how systems actually work.
Our outputs are shaped by operational reality, not only by what policy prescribes or theory recommends. When STAND designs a coordination mechanism or a training curriculum, it is built for the institutions, personnel, and conditions that will actually use it.
04
We combine technical depth with institutional fluency.
STAND is equally credible in a donor briefing room, a law-enforcement training environment, a municipal coordination meeting, and a community-based assessment. That range, held without compromising substance, is what makes STAND a trusted partner across sectors.
Where we started. Where we are now.
STAND began in 2018 in Cape Town, emerging from frontline community engagement and a clear-sighted recognition that the systems meant to protect the most vulnerable people were systematically failing them.
Over time, STAND evolved from community-rooted practice into a highly credible implementation and systems-strengthening partner with growing regional and international relevance. Its earlier community-based treatment work was run in research partnership with the South African Medical Research Council. The work deepened. The evidence base expanded. The institutional relationships strengthened.
Today STAND holds a distinctive position: the credibility of a senior technical partner with the agility of a specialist, senior-led organisation. It is led by

, a strategist, qualitative researcher, technical writer, and systems adviser whose career has been built at the intersection of gender justice, substance-related harms, and institutional accountability.

Flagship Programme
Track Her Case.
A survivor's journey through the justice system should never disappear in the handover between the services meant to protect her.
Gender-based violence systems rarely fail at the policy. They fail where one service hands her over to the next.
Across South Africa and the wider region, the mandates, laws and services to respond to gender-based violence (GBV) largely exist. What breaks is the coordination between them. A report opened with law enforcement stalls before it reaches the prosecutor. A survivor referred for health care is lost before social development ever hears of her. Information degrades as it passes between institutions, so that by the time a case reaches court it is often incomplete or illegible, and is struck from the court roll for want of clarity rather than merit. Each part of the system does its job, and the woman still falls through the spaces in between.
Track Her Case is STAND's response: a privacy-conscious way to follow a case across every institution that touches it, from first report through health, social services, policing and the courts, so that no handover becomes a dead end and the information holds together at each step. It makes the breakdowns visible and strengthens accountability across the whole system, while protecting the survivor's privacy and working within each department's existing mandate.
The Red Dot Process: live and working in the Overstrand
The Red Dot Process is the working pilot of Track Her Case, operating now in the Overstrand.
Designed by STAND in collaboration with Overstrand Municipality and the Overberg Violence Prevention Unit, with the support of the DG Murray Trust, the Red Dot Process is a seven-stage, privacy-conscious GBV track-and-trace workflow built onto Overstrand Municipality's existing Citizen App. The GBV reporting function went live on 24 August 2026. The first incident was dispatched to law enforcement (the South African Police Service) on 27 August 2026.
GBV Incident, live on the Citizen App
One tap to medical or police help
How it works
Seven stages, one non-identifying reference number
A report is logged through the municipality's existing Citizen App, with no new system for anyone to learn
The case is assigned a non-identifying reference number, so it can be tracked without sharing client records or names
Statutory partners were convened through the Western Cape Violence Prevention Unit to map legal mandates, response timelines, and handovers
Handovers are defined across law enforcement and its specialised Family Violence, Child Protection and Sexual Offences (FCS) units, the Department of Justice, the National Prosecuting Authority, the Department of Health, Hermanus Hospital, the Department of Social Development, child protection non-profit organisations and municipal structures
The Violence Prevention Unit signed off the workflow; Health confirmed its stage in the pathway
A trained community layer, embedded in the Department of Social Development's existing GBV Ambassador Programme, supports survivors and peer-led safety systems
The case is traced to resolution, producing a replicable, district-level record of where justice moves and where it stalls
Tap and zoom to read each stage
Built on what already exists
The design discipline is deliberate. The Red Dot Process adds no parallel database, requires no institution to change its mandate, and shares no identifying client records. It works through the systems that partners already use, and the roles they already hold. That is why it went from design to a live dispatch to law enforcement inside a single programme cycle.
The community layer is equally grounded. STAND equipped and further trained 31 screened GBV ambassadors within the Department of Social Development's existing GBV Ambassador Programme, supported testing and peer-led safety systems, and documented a replicable district model.

Governance
Board of Directors
STAND is governed by directors who bring substantive expertise in criminal justice, public law, drug-policy reform, harm reduction, and human rights, grounded in implementation experience and international engagement.
Accountable governance. Independent oversight.
STAND is a registered South African Non-Profit Company and Public Benefit Organisation, governed by a formal Board of Directors that provides independent oversight, strategic guidance, and fiduciary accountability.
The Board is deliberately constituted to reflect the technical and policy domains in which STAND operates, so that governance is substantively informed as well as procedurally sound. Members are selected for sector expertise, independence, and their capacity to strengthen STAND's institutional credibility and strategic direction. The Board oversees STAND's programme strategy, financial governance, risk and organisational development, with regular engagement between formal meetings.

         Four interconnected focus areas 
         STAND's work is intersectional by design: gender-based violence, substance-related harms, justice and health meet at the same point. We deliver across four technically demanding areas where the distance between evidence and action is longest. 
           Gender-Responsive Justice   Substance-Related Harms   Systems Strengthening   Alternatives to Incarceration  
       
     

     
       
         
           
            Focus Area 01  Gender-Responsive Justice and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Systems Strengthening  
         
         
            Who this is for  Government departments, municipalities, law-enforcement agencies, civil society organisations, and international partners working to strengthen institutional responses to gender-based violence.  
            What STAND delivers  STAND supports the design and strengthening of gender-responsive justice systems, from referral-pathway mapping and inter-agency coordination to law-enforcement training, survivor-centred protocol development, and district-level accountability mechanisms.  Specific work includes multi-sector GBV coordination support, law-enforcement and multi-agency training design and facilitation, referral-pathway assessment and redesign, GBV accountability-system development, technical writing and policy drafting, and institutional capacity building for frontline and management personnel.  
            What distinguishes our approach  STAND combines survivor-centred principles with implementation realism. We do not produce frameworks that cannot function in real institutions. Our outputs are tested against the actual capacity, culture, and constraints of the systems that will use them.

            Focus Area 02  Evidence-Informed Responses to Substance-Related Harms  
         
         
            Who this is for  Health and social-service providers, justice-sector partners, municipalities, and funders working to improve outcomes for marginalised and underserved women, and for populations affected by the intersection of poverty and substance use.  
            What STAND delivers  STAND develops and supports public-health-oriented, rights-based responses to substance-related harms, with particular expertise in the intersection of substance use, gender-based violence, and justice involvement. We design for what the evidence and the context require, never defaulting to a single framework.

         Why it matters 
         The laws exist. The handover breaks. 
         
           South Africa has some of the strongest laws on gender-based violence (GBV) in the world. What fails is the handover between services. A woman reports to the police and her case stalls before it reaches the prosecutor. She is referred for medical care and social services never hear of her. 
           Track Her Case closes those gaps. Each case gets a private tracking number, so no names are shared, and every handover is followed to the end. 
         
          Why now.  South Africa has classified gender-based violence as a national disaster and committed billions to fight it. More social workers, more courts and more care centres mean more handovers, and every handover is a place where a woman's case can be lost. Track Her Case makes sure it isn't.

         How it works 
         Four steps, from a tap to a court outcome 
         
            One tap to report.  An ambassador or survivor presses the help button on the municipality's Citizen App. 
            Help is sent.  Police and an ambulance go straight to the location, and the case gets a private number. 
            Every handover is tracked.  The number travels with the case through the police docket, the hospital and social services. 
            Followed to court.  Each appearance is tracked until the case reaches an outcome, so we can see where the system holds and where it needs strengthening.

How it works




Why now
South Africa has classified gender-based violence as a national disaster and committed billions to more
