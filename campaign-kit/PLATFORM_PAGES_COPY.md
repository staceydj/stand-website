## GlobalGiving

**Project title**
Track Her Case: follow every GBV case to justice

**Summary**
In the Overstrand, South Africa, Track Her Case follows every reported case of violence against a woman through the police, health, social services and the courts, until it is resolved. Each case gets a private tracking number, so no names are shared, and every handover is visible. The system went live on 24 August 2026 and dispatched its first case to the police three days later. During the 16 Days of Activism we are raising funds to keep it running through the festive season and to take it across the district. $10 keeps it running for more than an hour.

**Challenge**
South Africa has some of the strongest laws on gender-based violence in the world, and in November 2025 the National Disaster Management Centre classified gender-based violence and femicide as a national disaster. What fails is the handover between services. A woman reports to the police and her case stalls before it reaches the prosecutor. She is referred for medical care and social services never hear of her. Cases reach court incomplete and are struck from the roll. More social workers, courts and care centres mean more handovers, and every handover is a place where a woman's case can be lost.

**Solution**
Track Her Case, built by STAND with Overstrand Municipality and the Overberg Violence Prevention Unit, with the support of the DG Murray Trust and AIDSFONDS, works through the systems that already exist. A woman or a trained ambassador presses the GBV button on the municipality's Citizen App. Police and an ambulance are sent to the location, and the case gets a private number. That number travels with the case through the police docket, the hospital and social services, and every court appearance is tracked until the case reaches an outcome. 31 screened volunteer community ambassadors walk alongside women, equipped with training, phones, data and transport support to get survivors to the police, clinics, social services and court. No institution changes its mandate, and no identifying data is shared.

**Long-term impact**
Track Her Case produces a district-level record of where justice moves and where it stalls, so that police, health, social development and the courts can fix the handovers that fail. Phase 1 is built and live in the Overstrand, home to more than 130,000 people. Phase 2 takes it across the district, and the model is designed to be adopted by any district in South Africa and across the Global South.

**Story** (your own words, from the film)

> The first woman I met was in a rural town. She was blind, and she was being beaten so hard by her partner that she didn't know where the blows were coming from. A neighbour helped her get to the police station. The officer looked at them and told her to come back when she can see again.
>
> The second woman lived across the road from me. I heard her screaming and saw her partner ramming her head into her car. I called the police four times, five times. When they finally came, he told them everything was fine, and they left.
>
> Two women with nothing in common. Yet the system sent them both home.
>
> So we built something, with Overstrand Municipality and the Violence Prevention Unit. A woman can now report violence from her phone, and her case gets a private tracking number. Every handover, through the police, the hospital, social services and the courts, is visible. It is tracked. It is live, and it is working.
