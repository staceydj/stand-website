Track Her Case · Campaign

When a woman asks for help, the system should answer.

We built a live system in the Overstrand that follows every reported case of violence against women through the police, health, social services and the courts until it is resolved. Help us keep it running and take it across the district.

Give nowHow it works

The laws exist. The handover breaks.

South Africa has some of the strongest laws on gender-based violence (GBV) in the world. What fails is the handover between services. A woman reports to the police and her case stalls before it reaches the prosecutor. She is referred for medical care and social services never hear of her. Cases reach court incomplete and are struck from the roll.

Track Her Case closes those gaps. Each case gets a private tracking number, so no names are shared, and every handover is followed to the end.

How it works

  1. One tap to report. An ambassador or survivor presses the help button on the municipality's Citizen App.
  2. Help is sent. Police and an ambulance go straight to the location, and the case gets a private number.
  3. Every handover is tracked. The number travels with the case through the police docket, the hospital and social services.
  4. Followed to court. Each appearance is tracked until the case reaches an outcome, so we can see where the system holds and where it needs strengthening.

Designed by STAND with Overstrand Municipality and the Overberg Violence Prevention Unit, with the support of the DG Murray Trust and AIDSFONDS.
