Why it matters

The laws exist. The handover breaks.

South Africa has some of the strongest laws on gender-based violence (GBV) in the world. What fails is the handover between services. A woman reports to the police and her case stalls before it reaches the prosecutor. She is referred for medical care and social services never hear of her.

Track Her Case closes those gaps. Each case gets a private tracking number, so no names are shared, and every handover is followed to the end.

Why this December. The festive season is the most dangerous time of year for women in South Africa, and it is when the Overstrand is at its fullest. Violence against women happens behind every kind of front door: in farmhouses and townhouses, holiday homes and family homes. If you live here, holiday here or have family here, this is your community.

Why now. South Africa has classified gender-based violence as a national disaster and committed billions to fight it. More social workers, more courts and more care centres mean more handovers, and every handover is a place where a woman's case can be lost. Track Her Case makes sure it isn't.

How it works

Four steps, from a tap to a court outcome

  1. One tap to report. An ambassador or survivor presses the help button on the municipality's Citizen App.
  2. Help is sent. Police and an ambulance go straight to the location, and the case gets a private number.
  3. Every handover is tracked. The number travels with the case through the police docket, the hospital and social services.
  4. Followed to court. Each appearance is tracked until the case reaches an outcome, so we can see where the system holds and where it needs strengthening.

How your money is used

  • A case coordinator who follows every case and steps in when something stalls
  • Programme leadership that keeps police, prosecutors, health and social services working together
  • Support for our volunteer ambassadors: training, phones, data and transport to get women to the police, clinics, social services and court
  • Training for police and service providers, and community awareness so women know how to reach help
  • Independent analysis of outcomes, and an annual independent audit

Our ambassadors are volunteers: there are no stipends. STAND is lean and senior-led by design.

Built to scale

Track Her Case runs on systems that already exist: the municipality's app, the police docket, the hospital record and the court roll. So it can be taken into a new district without building anything new.

Phase 1 is built and live in the Overstrand. Phase 2 takes it across the Overberg district, and the Western Cape Violence Prevention Unit has confirmed its support for extending the work into other priority areas. Your support keeps a working system running this December, and builds the case for every district that needs it.
